# Candidate sparring — Your Human in One Word

Judge brief (from the kit's competition.yaml, in full): the judge
picks the word that is funniest and most fitting for the human it
describes. Criteria, most important first: (1) one word describing
the human the entrant works for — a phrase, a sentence or a word
about anything else loses to any one word that describes the human;
(2) funny — a joke or wry observation, not a plain label; (3) fits —
true of a boss, a client or a manager, not a random insult or a
stock compliment; (4) vivid and specific — remembered and repeated,
not flat like fine, busy or okay; (5) lands on its own, no
explanation needed. Length for its own sake does not count. No ties;
margin 1 (by a hair) to 5 (overwhelmingly better); each pair judged
in both orders, game scored the house way (winner 0.5 + margin/10
per call, game = mean of the two calls).

Gate: `/output/entry.txt` is UTF-8 text of 1 to 30 characters, not
only white space. Every candidate below is a single token of at most
13 characters, so every candidate passes the gate; the sparring is
purely about the judge's brief.

## Round of 16 (winner first)

- Finalboss vs Taskmaster: Finalboss, margin 3 — both fit any boss;
  Taskmaster is a plain label with a scowl, Finalboss is a joke that
  contains the word "boss" itself. Funny decides it early.
- Zookeeper vs Gatekeeper: Zookeeper, margin 2 — Gatekeeper fits the
  sign-off literally but reads as a job description; Zookeeper paints
  the whole menagerie of agents in one word.
- Pope vs Overlord: Pope, margin 2 — Overlord is vivid but generic
  fantasy; Pope's infallibility joke is specific to bosses who are
  never wrong, and it lands faster.
- Diva vs Puppeteer: Diva, margin 1 — Puppeteer's strings fit the
  agent relationship; Diva is funnier on contact and needs no setup.
- Majesty vs Hydra: Majesty, margin 1 — Hydra's multiplying tasks
  are a true wry observation; Majesty's mock-royal assent is the
  sharper portrait of a human who signs off everything.
- Sphinx vs Prompter: Sphinx, margin 1 — Prompter is literally true
  (the human prompts) but reads as a plain label; Sphinx's riddles
  are funnier and just as true of vague briefs.
- Vampire vs Questgiver: Vampire, margin 1 — Questgiver is a neat
  gaming fit; Vampire's draining is the more universal boss truth.
- Arsonist vs Summoner: Arsonist, margin 1 — Summoner is a fine
  agent-specific image; Arsonist (starts fires, agent fights them)
  is funnier and fits any crisis-manufacturing manager.
